\begin{abstract}
A language \(L\) is said to be \(\CC\)-measurable, where \(\CC\) is a
 class of languages, if there is an infinite sequence of languages in
 \(\CC\) that ``converges'' to \(L\).
In this paper, we investigate the measuring powers of \(\sf\) the class of all star-free languages and \(\group\) the class of all group languages.
It is shown that a language \(L\) is \(\sf\)-measurable if and only if \(L\) is \(\gd\)-measurable, where \(\gd\) is the class of all generalised definite languages (a more restricted subclass of star-free languages).
Moreover, we give a purely algebraic characterisation of \(\sf\)-measurable regular languages, which is a natural generalisation of Sch\"utzenberger's theorem.
We also show that the ``probabilistic independence'' of star-free and group languages, which is an important application of the former result.
Finally, while the measuring power of star-free and generalised definite languages are equal, we show that the situation is the opposite for subclasses of group languages:
for any two local subvarieties \(\CC \subsetneq \CD\) of group languages, we have \(\{ L \mid L \text{ is } \CC\text{-measurable}\}
\subsetneq \{ L \mid L \text{ is } \CD\text{-measurable}\}\).
\end{abstract}
