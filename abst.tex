\begin{abstract}
A language \(L\) is said to be \(\CC\)-measurable, where \(\CC\) is a
 class of languages, if there is an infinite sequence of languages in
 \(\CC\) that ``converges'' to \(L\).
In this paper, we investigate the measuring powers of \(\sf\) of the class of all star-free languages.
It is shown that the measuring power of star-free languages coincides with the measuring power of generalised definite languages (a more restricted subclass of star-free languages), which implies the decidability of \(\sf\)-measurability for regular languages.
We also show that the ``probabilistic independence'' of star-free and group languages, which is an important application of the former result.
